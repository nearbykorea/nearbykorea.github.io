THE PEOPLE EDIT · WATCH, COOK, READ

Good people to get curious with.

British creators and Korean food voices with a UK connection, opening a door to Korean culture.

By Korea, Nearby · Published 10 November 2026

IN THIS STORY

01 · Watch02 · Cook03 · ReadMore ways to learn ↗

Sometimes the easiest way into a culture is through someone else’s curiosity. A meal shared on screen, a sauce explained in a home kitchen, a story tucked into a cookbook: each gives you something to follow up in your own neighbourhood.

Here are three starting points with a UK connection. British creators sit alongside Korean food voices working in London; each offers a different kind of introduction.

J&O

01 / WATCH
British creators · UK & South Korea

Korean Englishman 영국남자

Josh and Ollie bring British and Korean culture together through food, travel and encounters with friends and guests. Their official biography describes filming in both the UK and South Korea, with Korean Englishman and JOLLY as their two channels.

Our starting point: choose a Korean Englishman food episode and keep a note of one dish that catches your attention. Look up the Korean name as well as the English description; it makes finding the dish on a menu much easier.

SH

02 / COOK
Korean food creator · London connection

Seji Hong & BOMBOM

Seji’s BOMBOM story begins with Korean supper clubs in her Wimbledon flat and collaborations around East London. It grew into a sauce brand and the cookbook Korean Made Easy. Her work connects Korean flavours with everyday cooking.

Our starting point: browse the BOMBOM recipe collection, then choose a dish that fits the equipment and ingredients you already have. It is a brand’s recipe site, so some recipes feature its own products; read the ingredient list before you shop.

Profile source: Seji’s first-person story ↗. This is a creator and food-business feature.

SS

03 / READ
Korean-born food writer · London-based

Su Scott

London-based writer Su Scott brings Korean food into home kitchens through recipes and personal storytelling. Her books include Rice Table and Pocha. Her agency biography describes a cooking style rooted in the food of her childhood.

Our starting point: spend time with the stories around a recipe. A cookbook can help you understand how dishes fit into everyday life, as well as how to make them. Check your local library catalogue if you would like to explore a book before buying it.
